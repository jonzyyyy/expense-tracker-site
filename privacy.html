Privacy Policy

Last updated: 20 November 2026

Sift is a personal finance app that runs on your device and, if you use iCloud, syncs through your own iCloud account. This policy explains what the app does and does not do with your information.

Summary. Sift does not collect your personal or financial data, and the developer runs no servers. There is no account, no login, and no analytics. Your data lives on your device and, if you're signed in to iCloud, in your own private iCloud storage — which only you can access.

What the app stores

All data you enter or import — transactions, amounts, merchants, categories, budgets, split bills and the people on them, subscription records, and any receipt photos attached to transactions — is stored in a private database on your device, inside the app's shared container. This data is:

  • never uploaded to us or to any third party,
  • never used for advertising or profiling,
  • accessible only to you, on your devices,
  • covered by your device's own encryption and passcode protection.

Receipt photos

When you add a transaction with Add from Receipt, whether by taking a photo or picking one, the photo is read on your device and then saved with that transaction so you can look at it later. You can also attach or remove a receipt photo when editing a transaction. Photos are shrunk before they're stored. Deleting the transaction, or removing its photo, deletes the photo, including from iCloud if you sync.

People you split bills with

When you split a bill, you can type a person's name or choose them from your contacts. Choosing uses Apple's contact picker, so Sift never has access to your contacts list and doesn't ask for Contacts permission. It only receives the one contact you tap, and keeps that person's name, phone number and a reference to the contact on the split. The name is used to tell friends apart and to match their payments to you. The phone number is used to address a payment request. These details are stored and synced like the rest of your data, and are deleted with the split.

If you send a payment request, Sift opens Apple's Messages screen with the message filled in, which can include the amount and your PayNow QR code. Nothing is sent until you tap Send, and the message goes through Messages, not through Sift or the developer.

iCloud sync

If you're signed in to iCloud, Sift keeps your data in step across your devices using Apple's CloudKit. Your data, including receipt photos, is stored in your own private iCloud database, tied to your Apple Account. The developer cannot see, access, or download it. Your currency and other preferences sync through iCloud as well. iCloud storage is provided by Apple and governed by Apple's privacy policy; it counts toward your iCloud storage.

To keep your data on a single device only, turn off iCloud for Sift in the Settings app (tap your name, then iCloud), or sign out of iCloud.

Deleting the app removes the data on that device. If you use iCloud sync, the copy in your iCloud remains until you delete the transactions in the app, or remove Sift's data in iCloud settings (Settings → your name → iCloud → Manage Storage). If you back up your device to iCloud or a computer, the app's data may also be included in that backup.

Processing that happens on your device

The following features process your information locally and send nothing anywhere:

  • Receipt scanning. Receipt photos are read using Apple's on-device Vision text recognition. Neither the photo nor the recognised text is sent to us or to any text-recognition service. The photo is kept with its transaction, as described above.
  • Statement import. PDF statements you import are parsed on your device. The file's contents are not transmitted.
  • Payment notification capture. When you use the app's optional Shortcuts automation, the notification text is passed to the app and parsed on your device.
  • Categorisation. Merchant matching and category suggestions run locally against keyword lists stored in the app.

The one network request the app makes itself

Apart from iCloud sync, which Apple handles, Sift makes one kind of network request.

When you record a transaction in a currency other than your home currency, the app looks up the historical exchange rate from Frankfurter, a free public API over European Central Bank reference rates.

That request contains only a date and two three-letter currency codes — for example, "what was EUR to SGD on 2026-05-14?". It does not include the transaction amount, the merchant, the category, any account details, or any identifier for you or your device. The conversion itself is calculated on your device after the rate comes back.

If you never enter a foreign-currency transaction, this request is never made.

In-app purchases

Sift is free to download. If it offers optional in-app purchases, they are processed entirely by Apple through the App Store. The developer never receives your payment details or your name, and doesn't link purchases to anything you've recorded in the app.

What the app does not do

  • No user accounts, sign-in, or email collection.
  • No access to your contacts list; only a contact you pick is seen.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash-reporting SDKs, or usage tracking.
  • No advertising or advertising identifiers.
  • No third-party trackers or data brokers.
  • No connection to your bank; the app never asks for banking credentials and cannot access your accounts.
  • No selling or sharing of data, because no data is collected.

Asking for your bank to be supported

If your bank isn't supported for statement import, Bank not listed? Ask for it lets you email a request to siftapp.support@gmail.com. Sift fills in the email in your mail app with the bank name, any note you add, and the app's version number. Nothing is sent unless you tap Send, and the email goes from your own mail account, not through Sift.

You can choose to attach a statement. A statement contains personal and financial details, so only attach one if you're comfortable sharing it. If you do:

  • it is used only to add import support for your bank, and is never shared with anyone else;
  • the developer may keep a test sample made from it, with your name, address, and account and card numbers removed (dates, merchants and amounts are kept, so the import can be checked against real totals);
  • the original email and statement are deleted once support for your bank is added, or once it's clear it can't be;
  • you can ask for it to be deleted sooner by replying to your email.

Children's privacy

The app does not collect data from anyone, including children under 13.
